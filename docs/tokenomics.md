## Separation of concerns

The protocol's accounting does not depend on a project token. The base amount and the grown portion are denominated in the funded dollar-pegged coin. A token, if one is issued, sits beside the protocol and cannot:

- be used to fund purchases,
- alter the grown portion of any holder,
- gain access to a holder's base amount.

This keeps the central promise, *only growth is usable*, independent of any market for a token.

## Principles for any future token

1. **Disclosure before distribution.** Supply, allocation and vesting are published, with the contract address, before anything is offered.
2. **No hidden mint.** Supply changes are only possible through a documented, on-chain, time-delayed process.
3. **Aligned unlocks.** Team and early-contributor allocations vest on a schedule long enough to outlast the first product cycles.
4. **Utility must be real.** Any right attached to a token must be something the product can deliver without touching holders' base amounts.
5. **Plain labelling.** No figure is presented as a return or a guarantee.

## Open questions

- Does a token exist at launch, or does the protocol launch token-less?
- If fees are charged, are they taken from growth only? (The design assumes yes: fees can never reach `base`.)
- Is there a rebate or reward mechanism for card usage, and who funds it?
- What governance surface, if any, is exposed to token holders?
